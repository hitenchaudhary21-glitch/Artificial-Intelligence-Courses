# Learning Resources
### *Your Complete AI Learning Toolkit*

> A curated, opinionated guide to the best free and paid resources for learning AI — from absolute beginner to cutting-edge researcher.

---

## 1. Online Courses

### Free

- **fast.ai — Practical Deep Learning for Coders**: Jeremy Howard's flagship course, available free at [fast.ai](https://fast.ai). Uses a top-down, hands-on approach — you train models before diving into theory. Best for: people who learn by doing.
- **Google ML Crash Course**: Google's free introduction to machine learning with TensorFlow. ~15 hours of video and exercises. Best for: absolute beginners who want a structured corporate-quality curriculum.
- **Kaggle Learn**: Free micro-courses on Python, Pandas, ML, Deep Learning, NLP, and Computer Vision. All exercises run in hosted Jupyter notebooks — no setup required. Best for: quick, practical skill boosts.
- **Coursera Audit (free)**: Most Andrew Ng courses (ML Specialization, Deep Learning Specialization) can be audited for free — you get full video and reading access without a certificate.
- **MIT OpenCourseWare — 6.S191 Introduction to Deep Learning**: MIT's official free deep learning course, updated annually. Covers CNNs, RNNs, GANs, and more with lecture slides and labs.
- **Stanford CS229 (YouTube)**: Full Andrew Ng machine learning lectures freely available on YouTube. Foundational theory including linear algebra, probability, and classic ML algorithms.

### Paid (Worth It)

- **Deep Learning Specialization (Coursera / DeepLearning.AI)**: Five-course series by Andrew Ng covering neural networks, CNNs, sequence models, and ML strategy. ~$49/month. Industry standard for structured learning.
- **Practical Machine Learning with scikit-learn (Udemy)**: Hands-on projects using sklearn — feature engineering, pipelines, model selection. Typically on sale for $15–20.
- **Full Stack Deep Learning (FSDL)**: Covers production ML systems end-to-end — data, training, deployment, monitoring. $0 (recorded) to ~$200 (live cohort).

## 2. Books

### 1. *Deep Learning* — Goodfellow, Bengio & Courville
**Level**: Intermediate–Advanced | **Free online**: [deeplearningbook.org](https://www.deeplearningbook.org)

The so-called "Deep Learning Bible." Dense, mathematically rigorous coverage of the full field — from linear algebra and probability fundamentals to CNNs, RNNs, and generative models. Not a beginner book, but the reference you'll return to for years.

---

### 2. *Hands-On Machine Learning with Scikit-Learn, Keras & TensorFlow* — Aurélien Géron
**Level**: Beginner–Intermediate | **Publisher**: O'Reilly

The most practical starting point for applied ML. Walks you through the full stack: data preparation with sklearn, classical algorithms, and deep learning with TensorFlow/Keras. Code-first, project-driven, and constantly updated.

---

### 3. *The Elements of Statistical Learning* — Hastie, Tibshirani & Friedman
**Level**: Advanced | **Free PDF**: [web.stanford.edu/~hastie/ElemStatLearn](https://web.stanford.edu/~hastie/ElemStatLearn/)

Rigorous graduate-level treatment of supervised and unsupervised learning from a statistical perspective. Essential for understanding what's actually happening beneath sklearn's `.fit()` calls.

---

### 4. *Natural Language Processing with Transformers* — Lewis Tunstall, Leandro von Werra & Thomas Wolf
**Level**: Intermediate | **Publisher**: O'Reilly

The definitive guide to the HuggingFace ecosystem. Covers fine-tuning BERT, GPT-2, T5, and more for classification, generation, question answering, and translation. Written by HuggingFace employees — authoritative and up to date.

---

### 5. *Designing Machine Learning Systems* — Chip Huyen
**Level**: Intermediate–Advanced | **Publisher**: O'Reilly

Focuses on the engineering reality of production ML: data pipelines, feature stores, model deployment, monitoring, and organizational challenges. Essential reading before your first industry ML role.

---

### 6. *Mathematics for Machine Learning* — Deisenroth, Faisal & Ong
**Level**: Beginner–Intermediate | **Free PDF**: [mml-book.github.io](https://mml-book.github.io)

Builds the three mathematical pillars of ML — linear algebra, calculus, and probability — with ML applications woven throughout. The best free math companion for someone starting from undergraduate level.

---

### 7. *Pattern Recognition and Machine Learning* — Christopher Bishop
**Level**: Advanced | **Publisher**: Springer

The canonical Bayesian ML textbook. Covers probabilistic graphical models, kernel methods, mixture models, and approximate inference. Extremely rigorous; favoured in academic and research settings.

---

### 8. *Grokking Deep Learning* — Andrew Trask
**Level**: Beginner | **Publisher**: Manning

Builds neural networks from scratch using only NumPy, with an emphasis on developing intuition before formalism. Ideal if you want to truly understand backpropagation before using a framework.

## 3. Research & Papers

### Where to Find Papers

- **[arXiv.org](https://arxiv.org)** — Preprint server for the majority of ML research. Key sections: `cs.AI` (Artificial Intelligence), `cs.LG` (Machine Learning), `cs.CL` (Computation and Language). Papers appear here before or alongside journal publication.
- **[Papers With Code](https://paperswithcode.com)** — Links papers to their official and community implementations. Also tracks state-of-the-art benchmarks across tasks. Essential for reproducibility.
- **[Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org)** — AI-powered academic search with citation graphs, author pages, and recommendation features. Better signal-to-noise than Google Scholar for ML research.

### Landmark Papers Every AI Practitioner Should Read

| Paper | Authors | Year | Why It Matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| "Attention Is All You Need" | Vaswani et al. | 2017 | Introduced the Transformer — the architecture behind GPT, BERT, and modern LLMs |
| "ImageNet Classification with Deep CNNs" | Krizhevsky, Sutskever, Hinton | 2012 | AlexNet — kicked off the deep learning revolution |
| "Generative Adversarial Networks" | Goodfellow et al. | 2014 | Introduced GANs; one of the most cited ML papers ever |
| "BERT: Pre-training of Deep Bidirectional Transformers" | Devlin et al. | 2018 | Defined the pre-train/fine-tune NLP paradigm |
| "Language Models are Few-Shot Learners (GPT-3)" | Brown et al. | 2020 | Demonstrated emergent capabilities at scale |
| "Playing Atari with Deep Reinforcement Learning" | Mnih et al. | 2013 | Deep Q-Networks — launched modern deep RL |
| "Deep Residual Learning for Image Recognition" | He et al. | 2015 | ResNet — solved vanishing gradients, enabled very deep networks |
| "The Bitter Lesson" | Richard Sutton | 2019 | Influential essay arguing that compute + general methods beat hand-crafted knowledge |

### Tips for Reading Papers

1. **Start with the abstract, introduction, and conclusion** before reading the full paper.
2. **Read the figures and captions** independently — they often tell the whole story.
3. **Use Papers With Code** to find reference implementations before re-implementing yourself.
4. **Keep a reading log** — even brief notes on what each paper contributes will compound over time.

## 8. Learning Path Recommendation

The resources above can be overwhelming. Here is an opinionated path from zero to advanced, using only resources from this guide.

### 🟢 Beginner Path *(0–6 months)*

**Goal**: Build intuition and practical skills. Write working ML code.

1. **Watch**: 3Blue1Brown — Neural Networks playlist (~1 hour)
2. **Read**: *Grokking Deep Learning* (Trask) — build intuition from scratch
3. **Course**: Google ML Crash Course — structured 15-hour intro
4. **Course**: Kaggle Learn — Python → Pandas → Intro ML → Deep Learning (in order)
5. **Tool**: Set up a Google Colab notebook and reproduce every exercise
6. **Community**: Join r/learnmachinelearning and fast.ai Forums

**Milestone**: Train, evaluate, and submit a Kaggle competition entry.

---

### 🟡 Intermediate Path *(6–18 months)*

**Goal**: Understand the theory. Work with real datasets and modern architectures.

1. **Course**: Deep Learning Specialization (Coursera / Andrew Ng) — full 5-course series
2. **Book**: *Hands-On Machine Learning* (Géron) — read cover to cover, run all code
3. **Course**: fast.ai — Practical Deep Learning for Coders (Part 1 and Part 2)
4. **Book**: *Mathematics for Machine Learning* (Deisenroth et al.) — fill in math gaps
5. **Papers**: Read landmark papers — start with Vaswani 2017, Krizhevsky 2012, He 2015
6. **Tool**: Migrate from Colab to local PyTorch; track experiments with Weights & Biases
7. **Follow**: @karpathy, @fchollet, @chiphuyen on Twitter/X
8. **Newsletter**: Subscribe to The Batch and Lilian Weng's Blog

**Milestone**: Fine-tune a HuggingFace transformer on a custom NLP task and deploy it with FastAPI or Gradio.

---

### 🔴 Advanced Path *(18+ months)*

**Goal**: Engage with research. Build production systems. Contribute to open source.

1. **Course**: Full Stack Deep Learning (FSDL) — production systems end to end
2. **Book**: *Designing Machine Learning Systems* (Huyen) — engineering reality
3. **Book**: *Deep Learning* (Goodfellow et al.) — the theoretical backbone
4. **Book**: *Pattern Recognition and Machine Learning* (Bishop) — Bayesian methods
5. **Course**: Stanford CS229 (YouTube) — rigorous theoretical foundations
6. **Papers**: Follow arXiv (cs.LG, cs.CL) weekly; use Papers With Code for implementations
7. **Watch**: Yannic Kilcher — pick a paper per week and watch the walkthrough
8. **Build**: A complete MLOps pipeline (data versioning → training → monitoring → retraining)
9. **Contribute**: Open a PR to a HuggingFace, PyTorch, or fast.ai repository

**Milestone**: Reproduce a recent arXiv paper from scratch, write up your findings, and share them publicly.

---

> **A final note on learning AI**: The field moves extraordinarily fast, but the fundamentals — linear algebra, probability, optimization, and the core architectures — change slowly. Invest deeply in foundations, and the latest papers will become much easier to read. Build things constantly. Share your work. The best AI engineers learn in public.
